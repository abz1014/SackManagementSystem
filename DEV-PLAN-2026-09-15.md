## 3. THE BUILD TRACK: STAGES TO FAT-READY

### FAT-ready, stated as an exit condition

A Factory Acceptance Test is **witnessable**: someone who is not the developer works down a written protocol on the development machine in one sitting and records pass or fail against each row. So:

> **FAT-ready means all six of the following are true.**
>
> 1. `FAT-PROTOCOL.md` exists, with one row per Definition-of-Done line and per phase acceptance criterion, each carrying an evidence artefact, reproduction steps, an expected result and a pass/fail box.
> 2. **Every row whose owner is "us" passes**, and its evidence is a committed file — a transcript, a printed specimen, a dated run — not prose.
> 3. **Every row whose owner is an IFL signature is marked as an open approval**, with the instrument named, the date it was sent, and what it gates.
> 4. **Every row that needs a plant host is marked "SAT, not FAT"**, with its dev-machine rehearsal transcript attached as the nearest available evidence.
> 5. **Zero open HIGH defects**, in a living register with a status column and a closing commit per row.
> 6. Every one of the assessment's sixteen §8 failures is either closed or **explicitly deferred in the protocol with a stated reason** — none is a surprise on the day.

Rows 3 and 4 are the design. A FAT that pretends the approvals and the host do not exist fails on the day; a FAT that names them converts them from failures into a **jointly owned checklist** — which is the deliverable's real value.

**Definition-of-Done lines that CANNOT be in the FAT-ready definition, because no plant host exists:** line 1's live-integration half; **lines 2 and 3** (reconcile against live source — no `ifl_live` generation has ever existed); **line 12**'s real-machine half; **line 13**'s host half (boot ordering is a host fact); **line 14**'s tamper-evidence half (it needs the `sms_migrate`/`sms_app` split performed at install).

**Lines that cannot be *met* by us at all, only requested:** 5, 6(b), 7, 9, 10, 11, and 8's signature half.

That leaves **lines 4, 6(a), 15 and 16 fully ours to close**, plus the code half of 1, 2, 3, 12, 13 and 14. That is the FAT-ready build set, and it is the plan below.

### My number, and where I disagree with the assessment

**The 3–4 week figure is optimistic by roughly half. My estimate for FAT-ready as defined above is 5–7 working weeks unscoped, and 4–5 weeks with the cuts in §8 — I would plan on five.** Four reasons:

1. **It costs the code and not the evidence.** A FAT is witnessed. Every "us"-owned row needs a captured artefact, and the assessment's own finding is that *"there is no stored transcript of any run anywhere in the repository"*. Producing, capturing and filing evidence is 25–30% on top of the code and is not optional.
2. **It costs items in isolation, not their integration.** The limit editor is costed at 2–4 days, but its hard part — an SMS-owned limit coexisting with a PDAS mirror that MERGE-overwrites every 60 seconds — is a data-model decision touching `product_limit_version`, `seedProducts`, `productLimits.ts` and roadmap rule 12. That is a design task with a build behind it, not a two-day feature.
3. **The integration harness is costed as if it passes.** Its entire purpose is to execute 34 migrations and every query string against a real schema **for the first time**. It will find things. Budget the findings, not just the harness: 1.5 weeks for the harness plus 3–5 days for what it surfaces.
4. **Agent parallelism does not compress the owner's serial time.** Seven agents write faster; one person still reviews, integrates and decides. The honest constraint is his hours, and that is the number the schedule should be built on.

**And therefore the right move is to cut scope, not to build faster.** §8 says exactly what I would cut. The cuts are what take 6 to 5.

## 6. THE `sms verify` PROBLEM

### What it does, verified

`sourceStats` (`cli/src/commands/verify.ts:206-215`) issues `SELECT COUNT(*) n, MIN([id]) lo, MAX([id]) hi, SUM(CAST([id] AS BIGINT)) s FROM [sourceTable]` — **the whole table, no predicate** — and `verify.ts:415-419` compares all four values exactly against the raw archive's stats for that epoch. The `SUM(id)` is genuinely clever: it catches equal-count-one-missing-one-extra, which COUNT alone cannot.

But the product's stated premise is that the sidecar keeps what IFL discards after about a month. **The day IFL prunes their first row, `src.n < raw.n`, `src.lo > raw.lo` and `src.sum < raw.sum` — all three — and `stop()` makes it exit 1 permanently.** The rows that trip it are the correctly-held archive rows that are the product's entire reason to exist.

And the diagnosis is wrong in a way that costs operator hours. `diagnose()` points at the worker's backwards gate, which (`sync-worker/src/runner.ts:172-181`, verified) tests `sourceMax < watermark` — **MAX(id) only**, which deleting old rows never moves. The operator is sent to hunt a halt that has not happened, during a cutover, in front of the client.

### What "reconciles" should mean

> **For the id range both sides currently hold, SMS's copy is exactly the source's; and for the range only SMS holds, SMS can say when the source stopped holding it.**

Reconciliation is the overlap plus an **accounted-for** remainder. Global equality was only ever true because both samples are unpruned snapshots.

### Options

| | Approach | Pro | Con | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Overlap window, computed per run.** Read the source's `MIN(id)`, compare only raw rows at or above it | No schema change, no new state, works today, works under any pruning policy | **Silently narrows itself.** Cannot distinguish "IFL pruned a month" from "IFL deleted a random block from the middle" or "the table was rebuilt". Leaves no record of what was pruned or when | Hours |
| **2** | **Archived floor per epoch, recorded.** Migration 037 adds `archived_below_id` + `archived_observed_utc` to `sms.source_epoch`. Each pass observes the source's `MIN(id)`; when it rises, raise the floor and stamp the observation. `verify` compares over `[floor, ∞)` and reports the remainder separately | Turns pruning from an anomaly into **a recorded fact with a date**. Gives the acceptance engineer the exact sentence they need. A **falling** MIN still halts — that is a reseed, not a prune, and the distinction is the whole point | One migration, a worker change, a CLI change, and a rule for the falling case | 1–1.5 d |
| **3** | **Period-scoped reconciliation.** `sms verify --from --to` over a production-day window | It is what an acceptance engineer actually asks for — *"reconcile last week against our own SELECT"*, FAT failure #8. Directly demonstrable in front of the client | Production timestamps are the plant wall clock with an ~18-minute acquisition lag, so a window has edge effects an id-range does not. Does not answer "is the archive intact" | 1 d |

### Recommendation

**Build 2 and 3. They are different products answering different questions, and both are needed.**

- **Option 2 is archive integrity** — *everything the source ever gave us, we still have, and what it has since dropped is accounted for.* That is DoD lines 2 and 3, and it is the one that fails at the worst moment. Build it first.
- **Option 3 is the demonstration** — the command that gets run at the FAT with IFL's own `SELECT` beside it. Build it second.
- **Fix the wrong diagnosis in the same change** (30 minutes). It costs the most operator time of anything here when it fires.

Reject Option 1 as a standalone: it produces a green result by quietly shrinking its own scope, which is the worst failure mode a reconciliation tool can have.

**Total: 2–2.5 days.** Stage D2.

### Which part is code and which needs IFL

**Ours, no IFL input, works under every possible answer** — the overlap arithmetic, the floor column and its observation, the falling-MIN halt, the corrected diagnosis, the `--from/--to` window, and a stored transcript of every run. **Build all of it now; none of it waits.**

**IFL must supply three things, and the third is the one people get wrong:**

1. **What they actually do** — prune in place, archive-then-delete, or rebuild the table. The three have different signatures and only they know. Option 2 handles all three; knowing which lets us say so in the protocol rather than hedging.
2. **The sample period** for the FAT demonstration. DoD lines 2 and 3 explicitly require one and none has ever been named.
3. **Acceptance of the definition.** And here is the pattern the whole client track should follow: **do not ask them what "reconciles" means — send them the sentence above and ask them to accept it.** An open question to a client returns silence; a sentence with a signature block returns a signature or an edit. Both are progress.
