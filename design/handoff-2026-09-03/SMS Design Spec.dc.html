Design spec
IFL Sack Management System · TP1 · Line 3 · Unit 2

A record of what the line made.

The existing app is a briefing note. This raises it to a ledger — a screen whose job is to prove a shift's output to the person who has to stand behind the number, and to be legible from the far side of an office.

The doctrine in app.css is kept: one typeface, one accent, rules not boxes, one column. Everything below is craft applied inside those rules, not a relaxation of them.

01
What changes

Three claims, and the change each one demands.

Kept
  • One typeface, tabular figures everywhere.
  • One accent, and it only ever means attention.
  • Rules, not boxes. No cards, no shadows, radii stop at 4px.
  • One column. Drill-downs open as a right-hand sheet.
  • Nothing measured against the browser clock.
  • Light only. One look, one contrast audit.
Elevated
  • Archivo → Instrument Sans. Same discipline, a far better figure.
  • The ramp gains a display step. A shift's output is the largest thing on the page.
  • Rules run full bleed; labels hang in a left margin. Structure you can read at a glance.
  • Figures sit on a shared baseline with hairlines between them, not in a bare 3-up grid.
  • Provenance is promoted out of Details into a permanent footer.
Added
  • The verdict: one ink-filled mark, for a figure that has been signed for. Nothing else may use a fill.
  • The wall becomes its own design — not the desk screen in viewport units.
  • A live pulse on the provenance line, so a frozen screen is visibly frozen.

Nothing here adds a colour, a panel or a metric. The two additions that are not typographic — the verdict mark and the wall layout — exist because the GM signs the shift number and reads it from a doorway, and neither job was served before.

02
Type

One family, six steps, and a screen may use at most four of them. Scaled by --ui-scale: desk 1, wall 1.3.

Instrument Sans
Variable 400–700 · tabular figures on globally

Why it replaces Archivo: a taller x-height at the same size, so 13px station labels survive a wall; unambiguous 1/7/9 at four metres; and a genuinely tight display cut, which is what lets one family carry both a 13px axis tick and a 132px wall figure. Archivo's tracking loosens at display size and its figures needed DM Mono beside them — the pairing this doctrine already retired.

The accent rule

Rust marks a thing that needs a person. It may never be used for emphasis, branding, a hover state, a chart series or a heading. Green survives only as the health dot. If a screen has no accent on it, nothing is wrong — and that has to stay true to be worth anything.

The one new fill

Ink at 100% behind white text is the verdict mark: a figure that has been signed for. It appears at most once per screen, only on Report and the active period control. It is not a button style and not a badge.

04
Structure

Rules run the full width. Labels hang outside the text.

The old page put a hairline and a 15px label at the top of every block inside a 1100px column, so each block announced itself the same way and the page read as one undifferentiated list. Two changes fix it without adding a single container.

Band

A block's rule spans the viewport, not the column. On a 2560px office monitor that line reaches the bezels and the page gains an architecture; the content stays at 1100px.

viewport-wide rule
content column · 1100px
Hanging label

The block label moves into a 180px left margin, level with the first line of content. The content column starts at a single left edge all the way down the page, and the eye can skim labels without reading through them.

One sentence, one size

The state is the largest text on the board at 4.1vw — 79px on a 1920 panel. It is the only thing readable from outside the room, and that is deliberate.

Stations become bars

Fourteen boxes with numbers inside are unreadable at four metres. Height encodes the count, the number sits below a rule, and a quiet station is a visible gap in the row.

The footer never leaves

Provenance and the last two readings are pinned to the bottom band. A display left on for a week must always say how fresh it is.

Failure is loud

Switch the state above to Plant link down: the pulse stops, the footer washes accent and the board refuses to claim a state. A frozen screen that looks live is worse than a blank one.
